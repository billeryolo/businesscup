ReadBuddy: a 10-minute daily learning app for parents and kids

A 1-minute check tells a parent whether their child reads and counts at grade level. Then 10 minutes a day, matched to the child's mistakes, closes the gap. Kids aged 3–10 practise letters, reading, numbers and math. The parent, the 25–40-year-old buyer, gets a verdict against school benchmarks, one thing to do tonight, and a plan.

Built from the billeryolo/businesscup repo (ReadBuddy: Next.js 16, Supabase, Claude) and the team's idea-selection chat: jury rubric, the "acute pain" filter, and Skills idea #8, "10 minutes of reading and math a day". The main market is the US, with benchmarks kept in a per-country table so other countries can follow. "ReadBuddy" is a working name.

The case for the jury

Why this idea, scored the way the jury scores

Say it in one line on stage: "Know in one minute if your child reads at grade level, then fix it in ten minutes a day, for less than two hours of tutoring a year."

Product & engineering40%

The reading check already works end to end with a microphone, a "parent scores it" mode and a demo reader. In 24 hours we add math and letters on the same pattern. Demo it live, with the fallback ready.

Value & retention20%

The child uses it daily and the parent opens it weekly for the check. The child moves through archetypes from age 3 to 10, so a family can stay subscribed for years. Compare the wedding planner, where churn is near 100% after the event.

Problem & user15%

A parent aged 25–40 with a child aged 3–10 who worries "is my kid behind?" The free check turns that worry into a number and a plan.

Business potential15%

$99 a year (or $14.99 a month) means about 1.01 million paying families for $100M ARR. The free check is the acquisition hook because the result is shareable. LTV beats CAC if a family stays more than one year.

Novelty10%

Learning apps teach. This one measures against school norms and writes practice stories at the child's exact phonics level.

The team's "acute pain" filter

PassLasts months

Years: learning to read and count runs from age 3 to 10.

PassAlready pay a specialist

Parents pay tutors and learning centres for the same skills, and school reading checks happen only a few times a year.

PartlyPain is visible now

Often invisible until a teacher or report card flags it. Our answer: the free 1-minute check makes it visible today.

PartlyNo cheap substitute

Free apps such as Khan Academy Kids teach well. None of them tells a parent "your child reads 62 words a minute; typical is 97; here is the plan." Pitch the measurement and the plan, not the games.

Avoid the Business Cup failure modes: one clear solution, a live demo instead of this page's detail, a real problem backed by norms, and a rehearsed delivery. The 48 requirements below are for the two of you, not for the slides.

AI layer

Where AI goes, and where it must not

Rules measure

Words correct per minute, accuracy, facts per minute, verdicts and percentile bands come from code and published norms. They must be repeatable and explainable to a teacher, so no model decides a score.

AI explains, generates and listens

AI turns numbers into plain advice, makes unlimited practice material at the child's exact level, and hears a child's voice better than the browser can. Every AI output has a rules fallback, as the parent summary already does.

Pitch suggestion: demo AI-4 live. Pick an interest ("dinosaurs") and a phonics pattern ("short a"), generate a story, show the checker rejecting a word the child hasn't learned, then read it aloud. AI-2 already works, so it is the safe second demo.
Two developers

Team split

Split frontend and backend. The backend developer builds every /api route, the scoring, the database and the AI, and tests them against a bare stub page. The frontend developer builds every screen against mocks. Both code against one file, src/lib/api/contract.ts, and connect at the end by flipping NEXT_PUBLIC_API_MOCK. The full list of requirements, pages and endpoints is on the API contract page.

Before the pitch

Risks and open questions

Benchmarks for letters and math factsReading has published norms. Letter naming and math-fact speed need a sourced table (e.g. DIBELS 8 for letter naming) before the app shows a percentile. Until then, show progress only.
Children's privacy (COPPA)Under-13 users need verifiable parental consent, and AI and speech vendors must be listed. Keep the rule that no audio and no child name leave the server.
Speech accuracy for young kidsBrowser speech mishears 5-year-olds. Keep "parent scores it" as the default for Pre-K and test a child-tuned provider before relying on it.
AI cost per familyCache generated stories by level and pattern so families share them, use low effort for summaries, and cap calls per family per day.
"There are free apps already"The jury will ask about Khan Academy Kids and similar apps. Answer: they teach; we measure against school norms and tell the parent what to do tonight. Keep the demo on the result page, not on the games.
Live demo failing on stageProduct is 40% of the score. Rehearse with the microphone, keep "Watch a demo recording" one tap away, and pre-seed a family with 3 weeks of progress so the chart isn't empty.
Exact standard wordingCheck the Head Start ELOF indicators and CCSS codes on the archetype cards against the source documents before they go on a slide.
Repo housekeepingdocs/SETUP.md still points at another repo and branch, and the README links a PLAN.md that isn't in this repo.
