# ReadBuddy: one-page pitch

## Who do we sell to?

**Parents of children aged 4–10 (Pre-K to grade 4) who want to know, at home, whether their child
is on track in reading and early math, and what to do about it.**

- **The problem is real and hidden from parents.** 88–92% of US parents believe their child reads
  at grade level; 40% of 4th graders read below NAEP Basic (2024, the highest share since 2002).
  Only about 30% of parents feel very confident they know what grade level requires.
- **Schools measure, but parents don't get the picture.** About 40 US states now require early
  reading screening, yet results reach home as a number without a plan. The products with real
  diagnostics (Amira, Lexia) are sold to schools, not to families.
- **The moment that matters:** a parent who just got a worrying note from school, or who simply
  wants to check before it becomes one. They want a fast, trustworthy answer and something to do
  tonight, not another game.

## What do we sell, and why do they come back tomorrow?

The parent pays for **a clear answer and a plan that works**, not for games:

1. A 1-minute check the parent can run anywhere, scored against the same published norms teachers
   use (Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017, DIBELS 8), with the mistakes it heard.
2. A verdict in plain words and a one-page report to bring to the teacher.
3. A **4-week path** to a realistic goal: one short practice a day, a re-check every 7th day,
   check days in the parent's own calendar, a streak. Home always says what to do next ("Today is
   check day: aim for 49") or "Done for today, tomorrow is day 9".
4. Practice matched to the result: research shows parents teaching a specific skill has about
   twice the effect of simply reading together (Sénéchal & Young 2008).

**Retention loop:** check → path → daily practice → weekly re-check → visible growth on the chart
("+11 words a minute in 3 weeks") → the next 4-week path. Growth targets follow real growth rates,
so progress is honest and visible, the strongest reason parents keep paying (low usage and cost
are the top reasons subscriptions are cancelled).

## Why does it make money?

- **Price:** $14.99 a month or $99 a year, 7-day free trial. In line with the closest consumer app
  (Ello: $14.99/month, $139/year); far below tutoring, which parents value at about $357 a month.
- **Cost to serve is tiny.** A reading check uses a few seconds of GPU (a T4 costs about $0.59 an
  hour, so roughly a tenth of a cent per check); the voice for games is pre-made audio; AI text
  costs well under a cent per request. No database, no accounts to host. Gross margin above 95%.
- **Market:** about 20 million US children are in Pre-K to grade 4 (approximate). $100M ARR is
  about 1 million families on the yearly plan, roughly 5% of that group, before other
  English-speaking markets or a school/tutor tier.
- **LTV > CAC:** a yearly subscriber is worth about $99–125; distribution is built into the
  product rather than bought:
  - **Teacher reports travel.** Every report a parent hands over shows a teacher a tool they can
    recommend to other families.
  - **State screening mandates** send millions of families home with a score each year; ReadBuddy
    explains it and turns it into a plan.
  - **Parent communities** (school groups, parent forums) share a free 1-minute check, which
    converts to the paid path.

3. Comprehension questions after the reading check.
4. A tutor and school tier using the same checks and reports.
